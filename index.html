Chapter 01 · The start

Where it started: a civil engineering diploma

It began with a three-year civil engineering diploma (a technical college program, similar to an associate degree) at Government Polytechnic in Ponnur, Andhra Pradesh, through the state board SBTET. Drawings, levels, concrete, soil — all the fundamentals I would lean on for years after. I completed it in 2018 with an 80.05% average (Distinction).

To keep studying, I moved in with my grandfather. Quiet evenings, a lot of reading, and a life far simpler than any site office I would see later.

Chapter 02 · Life at my grandfather's house

Fields, 104°F, and the people who work through it

While I studied, I also helped with farm work. I spent my days with the laborers and saw first-hand how hard they work in 104°F (40°C) heat — no shade, no shortcuts, and no complaints.

That respect for people who do the physical work never left me. It is why, on every site since, I check on the crew before I check the schedule.

Chapter 06 · Sree Geetha Constructions · March 2022 – July 2024

Running the details on real building sites

I worked with Sree Geetha Constructions, a building contractor in Andhra Pradesh, on the construction side of the work. This was where everything came together. I handled survey work, MEP coordination, RFIs, submittals, change orders, scheduling, crew management, plan reading, and inspection before concrete pours — and, for my last two months, MIVAN formwork.

Three projects: a 4-story building (about $700K), a 10-story building (about $6.6M), and a six-tower 41-story high-rise complex (about $240M total) where I worked with MIVAN from May to July 2024. (Values converted from Indian rupees, approximate.)

"The bars are on the bottom."

I flagged wrong rebar sizes, missing cover and missing minimum ties. Twice, on cantilever sections, the crew placed the main bars at the bottom instead of the top. I caught it both times and had it corrected before the concrete went in.

After that, I started staying with the crew whenever they work on a cantilever.

"No PPE, no work."

I caught workers who were not following safety rules. Some new laborers didn't listen to me directly, so I went to the contractor and said "no PPE, no work." After that, they followed.

Safety is the one thing I never negotiate on — and it is why I later earned my OSHA 30 and flagger certifications here in the U.S.

Reading the drawings one more time

Before starting any new section I read the MEP and structural drawings again. Then I hold a quick meeting with the MEP and structural subcontractors about how things are going, hand them their schedule, and adjust it where needed.

That ten minutes of coordination has saved far more time than it cost.

The last two months: MIVAN

In my final two months at Sree Geetha (May – July 2024) I worked with MIVAN, a reusable aluminum formwork system used to cast concrete walls and slabs floor after floor, on the 41-story towers. It was new to me, and I was glad to learn it on the job.

    Chapter 07 · A new country — August 2024

    Starting again, on purpose — and excited to

    I moved to the United States in August 2024 to earn my Master of Science in Civil Engineering, with a concentration in Construction Engineering Management, at The Catholic University of America in Washington, DC. I graduated in May 2026 with a 3.57 GPA.

    Alongside classes I worked in a university lab (February – May 2025) and as a waiter in the university restaurant. Nobody hands you a title in a new country, and I never minded starting from the beginning. I came here to learn how construction is built, inspected and managed in the U.S. — the codes, the standards, the way crews and inspectors work — and I am excited to keep growing here.
