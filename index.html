Chapter 03 · First internship

Widening an 18-mile irrigation canal

March – May 2018 · Site Engineer Intern · Government of Andhra Pradesh · Kavali

A state government project to widen and upgrade an 18-mile irrigation canal, raising its flow capacity from 250 to 450 cubic feet per second so farms downstream get reliable water. Budget about $1.4M, built in one-mile segments. I was a three-month site engineer intern.

I ran field leveling surveys with the auto level, recorded ground elevations for the canal alignment, and checked the numbers in the office against the design drawings and slope requirements. I studied the plans, cost estimates and quantity calculations, and documented canal deepening, shotcrete lining, retaining walls, check-dam repairs and culvert installation. It was my first look at civil infrastructure, survey instruments and government engineering paperwork.

Chapter 08 · Sree Geetha Constructions

Two and a half years, three projects

March 2022 – July 2024 · Project Engineer · Vijayawada, Andhra Pradesh

I started as an Assistant Project Engineer and was promoted to Project Engineer within seven months. Pick a project below.

4-story residential building

About $700K · March – September 2022 · Assistant Project Engineer

Coordinated the structural, electrical and plumbing subcontractors, suppliers, the architect and the client's representative. Ran quality checks before every concrete pour: reinforcement, formwork alignment, bar spacing and cover blocks. Tracked planned versus actual progress, slab cycles and floor-by-floor finishing. Managed RFIs, drawing reviews, work permits, inspection requests and QA/QC documentation, verified quantities and material procurement, resolved trade disputes and enforced daily safety.

10-story apartment building

About $6.6M · September 2022 – July 2024 · Project Engineer · 8 units per floor (one-, two- and three-bedroom)

The developer's own project, and I led the full lifecycle: earthwork, structure, MEP and finishes. I managed subcontractor coordination, bill approvals and meetings with the consultant and client, tracked more than 50 RFIs and submittals, and kept QA/QC logs and progress reports current throughout.

Two towers of a six-tower, 41-story complex

May – July 2024 (a short assignment) · Project Engineer · structural work only

The complex has six towers and is worth about $241M in total. It was not my company's project to run: Sree Geetha was a structural subcontractor, and the general contractor was a different company. My part was small and short: I was in charge of two of the six towers (about $48–60M of the work), for the structure only, for about two months before I left for the U.S.

On those two towers, which were at different stages (basement and superstructure), I supervised a team of assistant engineers, assigned tasks and reviewed their daily reports, controlled labor and ready-mix concrete billing, and quality-checked every pour. I also monitored MIVAN panel assembly, slab cycles and stripping schedules (MIVAN is a reusable aluminum formwork system that casts concrete walls and slabs floor after floor), coordinated with the general contractor's team, and kept the structural inspection records. MIVAN was new to me, and I learned it quickly.

How I work on site

"The bars are on the bottom."

I flagged wrong rebar sizes, missing cover and missing minimum ties. Twice, on cantilever sections, the crew placed the main bars at the bottom instead of the top. I caught it both times and had it corrected before the concrete went in.

After that, I started staying with the crew whenever they work on a cantilever.

"No PPE, no work."

I caught workers who were not following safety rules. Some new laborers didn't listen to me directly, so I went to the contractor and said "no PPE, no work." After that, they followed.

Safety is the one thing I never negotiate on — and it is why I later earned my OSHA 30 and flagger certifications here in the U.S.

Reading the drawings one more time

Before starting any new section I read the MEP and structural drawings again. Then I hold a quick meeting with the MEP and structural subcontractors about how things are going, hand them their schedule, and adjust it where needed.

That ten minutes of coordination has saved far more time than it cost.

The details that matter

Numbered panels, aligned ties, clean cover and safe access: a pour only goes well when all of it was checked before the concrete arrives.

    I moved to the United States in August 2024 to earn my Master of Science in Civil Engineering, with a concentration in Construction Engineering Management, at The Catholic University of America in Washington, DC. I graduated in May 2026 with a 3.57 GPA.
